## Slides: progress update

**Slide 1: What we did**
- Ported Nora's SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) into the new codebase alongside PPO, with the same environment, configs, logging and evaluation.
- Both algorithms output the same deployable 6 → 128 → 2 network, so either can run on the boat unchanged.
- Trained SAC on the new boat with **exactly PPO's task**: random goals 0.12–0.49 m away, domain randomisation (motor gain, deadband, delay, sensor noise), the same reward and seed.

**Slide 2: How SAC differs from PPO**

Both produce the same deployable actor: observation (6) → 128 → 2 thruster commands, tanh. They differ in **how they learn it**.

| | PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) | SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) |
|---|---|---|
| Data | **On-policy:** collect at least 4,096 fresh steps with the current policy, learn from them, then **discard** them | **Off-policy:** every step goes into a **replay buffer** (last 300k steps) and is reused many times |
| When it learns | once per batch: 10 passes over the batch in minibatches of 64 | **after every step**: one update on 256 steps sampled at random from the buffer |
| What the critic learns | **V(s)**: how good is this state? | **Q(s, a)**: how good is this action in this state? (two critics, and it trusts the lower one) |
| How the actor improves | raise the probability of actions that did **better than expected** (advantage), but limit each update with a **clip** (ratio within 0.8–1.2) so the policy can't change too much at once | adjust actions **directly in the direction the critic says is better** (gradient of Q through the action) |
| Exploration | Gaussian noise with a **learned, state-independent** std (one value per thruster), which shrinks as training goes on | Gaussian noise with a **state-dependent** std, plus an **entropy bonus**: the reward includes "stay random", weighted by α, which is tuned automatically |
| Stability tools | clipping, advantage normalisation, gradient clipping | target critics that update slowly (τ 0.005), two critics with the lower one used, automatic α |

**What that means in practice:**
- **Sample efficiency:** SAC learns from each step many times, so it needs far less sim time. It reached 90% (noise-free) after 53k steps; PPO used 1.28M.
- **Stability:** PPO's clipped, batch-wise updates change the policy slowly and predictably. SAC can be less stable: Nora's old-boat run collapsed mid-training, which is why we made the stopping rule stricter.
- **Wall-clock time:** SAC does a gradient update every step, so each step takes about 40% longer in wall-clock time.
- **Smoothness:** SAC's entropy bonus and per-step updates gave a smoother policy (action jumpiness 0.076 vs 0.14).
- **Noise at deployment:** both train with random actions but deploy the **mean** action. SAC's noise is part of what it optimises, so its noise-free behaviour can differ more from its training behaviour, which is what we saw with goals reached with noise but not without.

- **The approach speed is identical until about 0.25 m from the goal.** PPO then closes the final stretch faster: it reaches the radius at about step 37, SAC at about step 44.

**Slide 4: Learning speed at equal experience** (training episodes, median steps to goal)
- About 40k steps: PPO 68 vs SAC 81 steps.
- About 46k steps: PPO 57 vs SAC 96 steps.
- **SAC is more sample-efficient early on but plateaus.** Evaluation success stayed at 90–95% from iteration 14 to 42 while the training episodes were at 97–100%.
- SAC takes about 40% longer per step in wall-clock time (a gradient update every step).

**Slide 5: Where SAC fails**
- Its failures all happen **near the goal**: the boat gets within 0.10–0.16 m, then dithers (turns and reverses) just outside the 0.08 m radius.
- The stall happens with **1-step motor delay plus a deadband of about 0.1**: near the goal the policy switches to tiny, sign-flipping commands. The deadband swallows the small ones, and the delay applies each command a step late, so the corrections cancel out.
- With exploration noise it does reach those goals, so the noise-free policy gets stuck where a random nudge frees it.
- There are two kinds of failure: a **miss** (passes 0.16 m from the goal, swings out to 0.22 m, never recovers) and a **stall** (stops 0.10–0.12 m away while dithering with tiny, sign-flipping commands under a 1-step delay and a 0.1 deadband).
- The 18 successful episodes are very consistent (40–48 steps), and no failure happens on the approach. The weakness is the final 0.1 m.

**Slide 6: Bugs found and fixed along the way**
- **Episodes ran 1 step too long** (301 / 101 instead of 300 / 100): an off-by-one in the timeout check. It affected all past runs equally.
- **Ctrl-C lost the final weights:** stopping training skipped saving `final` and the summary. Fixed; recovered for this run.
- **The SAC training log** repeated stale evaluation values and counted timeouts as steps. Both are fixed.
- **Nora's earlier SAC run** (old boat) collapsed mid-training without anyone noticing. "Solved" now also requires the training episodes to pass, not just a few evaluation episodes.

**Slide 7: Why the real boat does worse than the sim** (real run `track_aruco_3d_20261117T193159`)
- **The wrong policy was deployed:** `ppo_spin_penalty`, trained on the **old 5 m boat** (goals 3–12 m away, 0.3 m radius, 20 m position scale). Its "spin in place, then dash" behaviour is the old boat's strategy.
- **The 0.4 action cap collapses each thruster to about 3 levels** (−0.4 / 0 / +0.4): turns become straight lines, and total thrust is 40% of what the policy had in sim.
- **The thrusters reversed about 25–30 times in 30 s,** and every step at the end. Each reversal costs about 100 ms of ESC brake and neutral in the firmware, which the sim doesn't model.
- **The control loop ran at 133 ms instead of 100 ms;** camera jitter and lost markers caused one-step action spikes.
- **It reached 0.079 m at step 129,** which counts as success in sim (0.08 m), but the real run used a 0.05 m radius.

## 1. Port to the new layout

- **Branch:** `nora/sac-v2`, made from `main`. Initial port committed as `5f46e2f`. `nora/SAC` is kept unchanged for reference.
- **[sac.py](../src/watercraft/algorithms/sac.py):** Nora's `training_scripts/gazebo_pool_v0_sac.py` rewritten in the shape of `ppo.py`. Select it with `"algorithm": "sac"` in `configs/train.json`.
  - The actor's mean is the same deployable 6 → 128 → 2 network, so `best.npz` and `final.npz` work with `evaluate` and `deploy` unchanged. The noise spread comes from a separate output head.
  - Two critics (6+2 → 256 → 256 → 1), each with a slowly-updated target copy (τ 0.005); the entropy weight α is tuned automatically, aiming for an entropy of −2.
  - Replay buffer of 300k steps; 2,000 random-action steps at the start; 1 gradient update per environment step.
  - Only a reached goal ends the future in the value target; a timeout doesn't (it bootstraps).
  - `latest.pt` stores everything needed to resume, including the replay buffer.
- **New:** `reward_scale` (0.1, the same as PPO). Nora's script didn't scale rewards.
- **Already on `main`:** Nora's `gazebo_env.py` change (wait for `/use_sim_time`), so it wasn't ported.
- **Kept as a record:** `runs/20261109-185357_sac_fixed_goal`. It was trained on the old 5 m boat, so its weights don't work on the new one.
- **Tests:** `tests/test_refactor.py` trains, resumes, exports and plots, and checks the tanh log-probability against PyTorch's own. 9/9 pass.

## 5. Findings so far

1. **SAC learns quickly but trails PPO at the same experience:** slower to the goal and not yet at 100%.
2. **SAC is about 40% slower per step in wall-clock time,** because it does a gradient update after every step.
3. **The noisy and noise-free policies disagree.** Training episodes (with noise) reach 97–100% of goals, but the noise-free evaluation fails 1–2 of the same 20 goals almost every time. The noise-free policy probably stalls or circles on particular goals, and a little noise gets it out. The real boat runs noise-free, so this matters.
4. **Evaluation success has stopped improving at 90–95%** since iteration 14 (6 evaluations in a row), while steps to the goal settle around 60–65. Training episodes are at 100% over the same period.
5. **No collapse like Nora's run.** The critic's values and loss are stable and α rises slowly (0.001 → 0.005). Exploration shrank early, though: std was about 0.13 from iteration 3 on.
6. **The reward barely rewards speed.** The distance reward totals the same whether the boat is fast or slow. Only the step penalty (0.01 per step after scaling) and discounting push for speed, so trip length can drift.

## 6. Mistakes caught along the way

- I first compared SAC's "117.55 eval steps" with PPO's "38 steps". Those aren't comparable: a mean including timeouts on 20 random goals, against a median over goals reached on one fixed goal 0.47 m away, from a finished 6-hour policy. The fair comparison is in the table in section 4.
- The run's CSV repeated evaluation values on rows without an evaluation, which looked like several identical evaluations. Fixed for future runs.

- **Stopping with SIGINT lost `final` and `summary.json`.** Ctrl-C (or `pkill -INT`) also shuts down rospy inside the training process, so the `finally` block's call to pause Gazebo raised, and the exception skipped saving. Fixed: `sim_env.stop()` now only warns if the simulator can't be paused, so cleanup always finishes (this covers PPO and cross-entropy too).
- **Off-by-one in the step limit:** the environment checked for a timeout before counting the step, so episodes timed out after `max_episode_steps + 1` steps (301 in training, 101 in evaluation). Fixed in `pool_gym_v0.py`. This affects past PPO and SAC runs equally, so the comparisons above still hold.

- The log's checkpoint SHA-256 (`a2ad…`) ≠ this repo's file (`3edb…`), so the copies differ.
- **Action:** deploy `runs/ppo_enph479_1116/checkpoints/best.npz`.

**Translation from network outputs to motor commands:**

| Issue | Evidence in the log | Sim |
|---|---|---|
| `max_action` 0.4 caps almost every output | step 1: [−0.99, +0.98] → [−0.4, +0.4]; step 156: [+0.98, +0.21] (left 4.7× right) → left 0.4, right 0.21, a much weaker turn; [+0.99, +0.47] → both 0.4, straight instead of turning | full ±1 |

| Log columns | `motor_left` / `motor_right` are ESP32 channels **after** `swap_channels`, so `motor_left` drives the physical right thruster | — |

**Behaviour:** spin in place ([−1, +1] for 10–15 steps), dash, repeat. Closed from 0.69 m to 0.079 m by step 129, spun away to 0.31 m, came back to 0.09 m at step 237, and left again.

**Fixes, in priority order:**
1. Deploy the new-boat policy and use a 0.08 m radius (or retrain with 0.05 m).
2. Remove the 0.4 cap, or apply the same cap in the sim during training.
3. Model the firmware in the sim: deadband jump to command 40, about 100 ms reversal delay, control period 100–140 ms. Penalise sign flips.
4. Filter the camera velocity; hold the last velocity after a lost marker.
